Privacy policy

Effective date: 2026-11-12

KnowTheNext is a geography quiz for Apple TV. This policy explains what data the app sends, why, and how to turn it off. In short: the app sends anonymous usage data so we can see how it's used and fix problems. It never sends your name, your Apple Account or anything you type, and you can turn it off in Settings.

Who is responsible

KnowTheNext is made by an independent developer, the seller shown on the app's App Store page. That developer decides what data the app collects and is responsible for it.

What the app sends

While you use the app, it sends these events:

  • How the app is used: which screens you open and which buttons you press, outside a game in progress. The app also sends when it's installed, opened and closed.
  • Finished and abandoned games: the group, which clues and answers you chose, the difficulty, the language for country names, your score, how long it took and which countries you missed. For a group you made yourself, only its size is sent, never its name.
  • The purchase: when the purchase page opens, and whether a purchase or restore was started, completed, cancelled or failed. No payment details: Apple handles the payment, and we never see them.
  • Crashes and errors: what went wrong and where in the app, so it can be fixed.

Each event comes with general details about the device: the model (Apple TV), tvOS version, app version, language, time zone and screen size. It does not include your Apple TV's name.

What the app never sends

  • Your name, email address or Apple Account.
  • Anything you type, such as group names or searches.
  • Your location. IP addresses are not stored, and no location is looked up from them.
  • Advertising identifiers. The app has no ads and does not track you across other apps or websites.

Anonymous, not personal

Each install of the app gets a random ID, so events from the same Apple TV can be seen together, for example to see whether people who finish the Starter group go on to buy the full app. The ID is not connected to you, your Apple Account or your Apple TV, and the app never asks who you are. Deleting and reinstalling the app gives it a new ID.

Why

To see which parts of the app people use and where they stop, so we can make it better, and to find and fix crashes. The data is not sold, not used for advertising and not shared with anyone for their own use.

Who processes it

The data is sent to PostHog, an analytics service, and stored in PostHog's EU cloud in Frankfurt, Germany. PostHog processes it on our behalf and may not use it for anything else. Events are kept for one year and then deleted.

Turning it off

Open Settings in the app and turn off Share anonymous usage data. From then on the app sends nothing, crashes included. You can turn it back on at any time.

Purchases

The one in-app purchase is made through Apple. Apple handles payment and your Apple Account under Apple's privacy policy. The app only learns from Apple whether you have the purchase, so it can unlock the full app.

What stays on your Apple TV

To remember your progress, the app keeps these on your Apple TV, in its local storage:

  • Your settings
  • Saved games
  • Custom groups you make
  • Your best scores

These are not sent anywhere. Deleting the app from your Apple TV deletes them.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to ask what data is held about you, and to have it deleted. Because events are tied only to a random ID and not to you, we usually can't find which events are yours. Turning off Share anonymous usage data stops any more from being sent. If you have a question or a request, contact us below. You can also complain to your local data protection authority.

Children

KnowTheNext is suitable for all ages but is not made specifically for children. It collects no personal data from anyone, children included.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new effective date.
